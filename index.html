About Me

The work of a conservation practitioner in the Philippines is rarely a straight line. It is a series of knotty problems where wildlife protection collides with institutional friction, local bureaucracy, and human survival. Untangling these complexities requires more than field implementation. It takes collective imagination to see value in what is hidden and deliberate design to turn passion into practical conservation governance.

Parallel to this field reality is a deliberate disconnect: while managing remote conservation programs, I am also completing a BA in Multimedia Studies at the UP Open University. This academic track exists alongside my field identity, a separate lens through which I navigate a career built entirely on the ground.

Increasingly, my work explores a broader question: how societies recognize, translate, and respond to ecological reality in biodiversity-rich but institutionally fragmented contexts, and how culture, technology, and public imagination shape what is seen, protected, or forgotten.

I am Kristoffer Noriel Ordoñez. You can call me Tope.

Things I Love

  • Field-based conservation that brings together many of the questions that hold my attention: how ecosystems persist, how people inhabit landscapes, and how knowledge informs decisions about their future. Working on the ground places these questions within the realities of livelihoods, local institutions, and ecological change. My interest lies in understanding those relationships and finding practical ways to protect biodiversity while recognizing the communities and cultural landscapes connected to it.
  • Reading and collecting books that let me pursue questions beyond the immediate demands of paid work. My interests extend into history, culture, and public life, where different accounts can deepen or unsettle an understanding formed through experience. Collecting books gives this inquiry a continuing physical presence. The growing collection also forms part of Balay Dagitab, a developing idea for a space that brings books, music, and film together.
  • Analog media, which includes film cameras, portable pre-cloud music players, and turntables, alongside a growing collection of records, CDs, and cassette tapes. I am drawn to the relationship between the material and the means of experiencing it: a photograph and its camera, a recording and its playback equipment. These formats remain part of my engagement with multimedia, connecting an interest in content with the objects and processes through which it becomes accessible.

Passion Projects

Operational

Sierra Madre Conservation Society

Founded to bring together the protection of ecosystems and the preservation of cultures. Its flagship initiatives include Found Nation, SierraCon, and the Sierra Madre Trail Project. Found Nation carries my particular interest in species that are lost, overlooked, or given little public attention, drawing on Indigenous and local knowledge to help guide scientific inquiry and protection. Together, these initiatives give practical direction to a work bridging biodiversity, cultural landscapes, and public engagement, with each addressing a different part of the relationship between people and the environments they inhabit.

2023

Developmental

Balay Dagitab

A developing House of Ideas, envisioned as an off-grid cabin bringing together a growing collection of books, recorded music, and film. Its development reaches back to 2013 and continues through the collection itself: books, vinyl records, CDs, and cassette tapes accumulated over time. The physical house remains to be realized, but the materials that would give it character are already taking shape. The project brings these interests into a shared setting, with the eventual space growing from an existing practice of collecting, reading, listening, and viewing.

2013

Conceptual

The Diamond Discourses

A three-year writing project, it examines Philippine sociopolitical struggle from 1968 to 2028. Work is already underway, with drafting and the assembly of available historical material. As the project moves toward 2028, that foundation will develop alongside the events that complete its intended period, bringing historical inquiry and a continuing political present into the same work.
